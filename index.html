1. The Paradox of Food Surplus & Scarcity

High food waste does not correspond to high hunger levels. Developed countries (e.g. Italy, Canada, USA, France, Spain) generate the largest waste per capita (exceeding 2,200 kg/year) and massive economic losses ($1.0M - $1.37M each), yet maintain low hunger scores (< 5.0).

2. Concentration of Severe Hunger

Countries suffering from high to extreme hunger severity (India: 33.2, Indonesia: 24.1, Ethiopia: 20.5, South Africa: 15.4) are plagued by distribution bottlenecks, post-harvest losses, and socio-economic instability rather than lack of global food supply.

3. Staggering Economic Loss

The cumulative economic loss across analyzed countries stands at $17.80 Million. Eliminating even 15% of food wastage in OECD nations could recover hundreds of thousands of tons of edible calories for vulnerable regions.

4. Category Breakdown Analysis

Tableau food category analysis reveals that Prepared Food (711 units), Beverages (639 units), and Bakery Items (630 units) constitute the top three sources of discard, representing rapid perishability and consumer over-purchasing in urban markets.
